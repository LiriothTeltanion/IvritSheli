# Ivrit Sheli product manifesto

## Hebrew should feel alive

Ivrit Sheli exists to turn Hebrew from something intimidating into something a
learner can notice, touch, hear, remember and use. The app begins with a small
success, not a form. It grows from three useful words into a personal map of
daily life.

## A journey, not a dashboard

The interface travels through Israel: Galilee paths, Haifa and Carmel, the
streets of Tel Aviv and Jaffa, Jerusalem stone, the Dead Sea and the Negev.
Each place supports a learning moment rather than serving as decoration. Words
belong to situations, people, sounds and memories.

## Three ways to travel

- **Guided** gives a beginner one clear next step.
- **Explorer** opens paths, connections and choices.
- **Experienced** makes depth and speed available without pretending that more
  controls mean more learning.

Learners can change mode. A mode is an interface preference, not a judgement
about intelligence or ability.

## Delight must earn its place

Illustration, motion, audio, achievements and playful surprises are welcome.
They should clarify meaning, reward real effort or make practice easier to
return to. The app does not punish mistakes, sell anxiety or manufacture
scarcity through hearts, energy or leagues.

## Evidence over theatre

Mastery comes from meaningful retrieval, correction, delayed review and real
use—not from screen time, XP or an AI-generated compliment. Advanced material
is labelled honestly. Browser transcription is not an accent score. A
recommendation explains why it appeared.

## Technology should expand possibility

Local rules, cloud accounts, AI, speech services and future mobile apps are
tools, not identities. Use the simplest reliable tool for the learner's need.
Experiment boldly, validate boundaries, provide a graceful fallback and keep
the product understandable.

## Trust stays in the foundation

The visible story is joy, progress and usable Hebrew. Underneath, account data
still belongs to the learner, workspaces stay isolated, secrets stay secret and
exports or deletion remain possible. Trust should feel natural rather than
turning every harmless action into a warning.

## The real acceptance test

A person who is new to both Hebrew and technology should be able to open a link,
learn three words and finish a session without assistance. An intermediate
learner should still find grammar, context, dictionary depth and meaningful
practice. Every major release should be tested against both needs.
